# Numind Full Content Audit — June 2026

**Date:** 2026-06-04
**Scope:** All 10 modules · 118 lessons · 686 quiz templates (433 numerical + 253 concept/decision) · 96 application items.
**Method:** A three-lens expert panel (Financial Educator · Product Manager · Learning-Behavior Specialist) audited each module against four criteria — **Relevance, Quality, Accuracy, Up-to-date** — working from a fully-expanded dossier of every lesson and its attached templates (prompt, variable ranges, formula or scenario+choices+correct-answer, difficulty, explanation). The lead auditor then **independently verified every high-risk 2026 regulatory number** against primary sources (IRS, SSA, DOL, Federal Student Aid) rather than trusting the corpus.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**Numind's content is strong, current, and genuinely decision-first.** Across 118 lessons the product does what it claims: it pairs a mental-math shortcut with a real money decision, and the application items consistently use distractors that map to real misconceptions rather than filler. Relevance is uniformly excellent. The regulatory currency is, frankly, better than most paid finance apps — the corpus even carries the **Parent PLUS 2026-27 interest rate that was published *today* (June 4, 2026)**.

**The headline accuracy result is reassuring.** Every 2026 figure I independently checked — tax brackets, standard deductions, capital-gains thresholds, Social Security wage base, 401(k)/IRA/catch-up/super-catch-up limits, the §415(c) cap, HSA/HDHP limits, FSA/DCFSA caps, the Parent PLUS rate and origination fee — **is correct.** Two figures the panel flagged as "highest accuracy risk" (the §415(c) `$72,000` cap and the Parent PLUS `9.07%` rate) turned out to be **false alarms — both are right.**

**There is exactly one confirmed factual error in the entire corpus:** the Student-Loans module teaches that unsubsidized interest **capitalizes at the end of the grace period**. A July-2023 federal rule eliminated capitalization when a borrower enters repayment. Interest still *accrues and is owed* during grace, but framing it as capitalization (interest-on-interest) overstates the harm and is now wrong. **This is the only P0 accuracy fix.**

The rest of the findings are not factual errors — they are **maintainability and polish**: pervasive near-duplicate templates (one formula reused with cosmetic prompt changes), a handful of internal contradictions (the same quantity stated two or three different ways across sibling lessons), several stale *market* estimates (credit-card APR, HYSA yield, car-insurance baseline) that need a "varies/illustrative" hedge and a refresh cadence, and a few computational lessons missing an application item.

**Overall grade: A− / B+.** Relevance 5.0 · Up-to-date 4.6 · Accuracy 4.5 (post-verification) · Quality 4.3 (duplication is the single biggest drag).

**Two items needing a content change as a result of verification:**

- ❌ **Student-loan grace-period capitalization** (`student_loan_grace_period`, `concept_sl_subsidized_vs_unsubsidized`): the 2022 final rule (effective 2023-07-01) ended capitalization when a borrower *enters repayment*. Reframe from "interest capitalizes / is added to principal at repayment start" to "interest **accrues and is owed**; on most Direct Loans it no longer **capitalizes** at the end of grace (capitalization now happens only at statutory triggers such as leaving the IBR plan or default)." The fix message — *pay the interest during grace* — stays valid; only the mechanism wording is wrong.
- ⚠️ **FLSA exempt status** (`concept_salary_exempt_status`): the `$35,568` floor is **correct**, but update the narrative ("the 2024 increase to $58,656 was vacated; DOL restored the 2019 regulation by technical amendment in May 2026 and dismissed its appeal") and either refresh or genericize the California `$66,560` figure (CA's threshold is 2× state minimum wage and rises annually).

Everything else flagged by the panel as an "accuracy risk" is a market-rate estimate, an internal contradiction, or a math-modeling choice — not a wrong fact. Those are catalogued below.

## 4. Cross-cutting findings

### Financial-Educator lens — accuracy & sound behavior: **excellent, with one fix**
- Regulatory numbers verified correct across the board (see §3). The team's discipline on YMYL is real: tax-year tagging, primary-source citations inline, and honest "we don't know your future" framing on Roth/IDR.
- The **single confirmed factual error** is the SL grace-period capitalization mechanism (P0).
- **Stale framings (not wrong numbers):** FLSA exempt narrative + CA figure (Salary); BNPL "9 loans/year" is 2022-23 CFPB data (Debt); the disability "1-in-4" stat carries three lessons in Insurance.
- **Behavioral soundness is strong** — no individualized investment advice, warranty/GAP correctly framed as negative-EV, refi-forfeits-protections hammered relentlessly, mortgage framed as a tool not a sin.
- **One understatement to fix:** employer-paid LTD benefits are taxable, so Insurance's "60% replacement" overstates true net (`concept_disability_supplemental_d3`).

### Product-Manager lens — relevance & decision-first positioning: **5/5 relevance, dragged by redundancy**
- Every module scored 5/5 relevance. The topic set is exactly what US adults mishandle, and the Life-Event-Pack framing lands.
- **Near-duplicate templates are the #1 product debt.** Examples the panel surfaced: `price_per_use` runs the same `costPerUse` formula 6×; balance×APR÷12 appears across 5 Debt templates; `moveInThreeMonths` (3×rent) fills 3 of 5 Housing move-in slots; `weekly_to_annual`≈`weekly_annual_d1` and three `annualFromHourly` twins (Salary); three refi-vs-PSLF subtractions (Student Loans); `tip_bill_total` is byte-identical in two Smart-Shopping lessons. This inflates the 686 count, makes the spaced-review queue feel repetitive, and is the main thing standing between Quality 4.3 and a 5.
- **Application-coverage gaps** (computational lessons with no "what would you do" item): `housing_true_cost`, `housing_move_in_cost`, `taxes_w4_adjust`, `car_loan_term`, `car_total_cost_ownership`, `student_loan_total_cost`. *Caveat:* several pure-math lessons (tips, latte-factor, weekly/monthly) are **deliberately** application-free per the Phase 9E fact-based-only directive — see Appendix. The six listed above are total-cost/decision-adjacent and are the genuinely debatable gaps.
- **A few internal contradictions** that read as bugs to a careful user: W-2-vs-1099 break-even stated 3 ways (Salary); min-payment "1%+interest" vs "2-3%" (Debt); buy-vs-rent opportunity cost at 10% vs 8% (Housing); FSA example $2,850 vs the $3,400 cap (Insurance); 2,000 vs 2,080 work-hours (Salary).

### Learning-Behavior lens — pedagogy & transfer: **strong scaffolding, a few mislabels**
- **Distractor quality is the app's pedagogical strength** — wrong answers encode named misconceptions (additive discount stacking, "carry a balance to build credit," "paid-in-full = 0% utilization," "lower monthly is always cheaper," withholding≠liability). This is what makes the MCQs diagnostic instead of decorative.
- **Concept→shortcut→example→practice coherence** is tight, and Salary/Debt/Investing show genuine difficulty laddering (single-variable d1 → multi-step d3 scenarios that mirror real statements).
- **Difficulty mislabels** to fix: `price_with_tax` tagged *hard* though it's the simplest op (Budgets); `subscription_compare_d2` *hard* vs the easier `sub_audit_annual_d2` *med*.
- **A few weak/cartoonish distractors** dilute diagnostic value: "any capital-gains sale is taxed at 50%" and "wage garnishment" (Taxes), `ins_decision_generic_d2` is a knowledge-check not a decision (Insurance).
- **Display bug:** `on_track_multiple_d1` renders a 1.23× savings multiple as "123%", clashing with the lesson's "×" language (Investing).
- **Redundancy hurts retention design too** — when five SRS items are the same formula, spaced practice degrades into rote repetition rather than varied retrieval.

## 7. Appendix — Phase 9E deliberate-exclusion context

When reading "lesson has no application item," note that **26 of 118 lessons were deliberately left application-free** in Phase 9E, per the directive: *add a decision item only where an objectively-correct, fact-based question exists.* These are pure mental-math lessons (tip math, sales tax, hourly conversion, Rule of 72, ROI, latte factor, weekly/monthly), near-duplicates, and volatile-policy lessons (`student_loan_idr`) where a fact-based decision item would be contrived or would drift. For those lessons the calculation *is* the skill, and the absence of an application item is correct.

The six application gaps called out as genuine in §4/§5 (`housing_true_cost`, `housing_move_in_cost`, `taxes_w4_adjust`, `car_loan_term`, `car_total_cost_ownership`, `student_loan_total_cost`) are total-cost/decision-adjacent lessons where a fact-based decision item *does* exist — they are candidates to close, not pure-math exclusions.
