# "Money skills school never taught you" — demand research & positioning

> **June 2026.** Three-agent web research (demand evidence / competitive
> landscape / K-12 mandate landscape) + an internal audit of our 127 lessons
> against the canonical "school never taught you" checklist. Companion to
> `product-strategy-audit-2026-06.md` and `search-demand-research-2026-06.md`.

## 1. The positioning sentence

**Numind teaches the real-life money skills school never taught you — so
when the decision shows up, you're ready.**

Three parts, all load-bearing:
- **School gap** = the origin story. Externalizes shame ("you're not bad
  with money; nobody taught you"). The resonance frame.
- **Money decisions / moments** = the stakes and the when.
- **Readiness** = the receipt — what the subscription provably delivers,
  made credible by honesty mechanics (Ready is earned; we say when you're
  not).

The frame alone is a commodity (every FinTok creator uses it). The frame
plus practice/readiness mechanics for paying US adults is **unoccupied**
(see §3).

Tax/seasonal urgency: soft awareness ("benefits season is around the
corner"), never countdown fear. Regret windows (post-tax-filing late April)
are *marketing timing*, not in-app pressure.

## 2. Demand evidence (five ways)

### Stated regret (strongest)
- **82%** of US adults wish they'd been *required* to take personal finance
  in HS; **91%** among >$100K earners; 83% want it mandated. (NEFE/SurveyUSA,
  Mar 2025, n=800)
- **70%** of adults who missed it say their quality of life would be better
  if they'd had it. (NEFE, Oct 2025)
- Only **33%** of Gen Z learned about money in school; 75% from family.
  (Bank of America Better Money Habits, 2025)
- Self-reported cost of financial illiteracy: **$948/adult in 2025**
  (~$246B nationally). Caveat: self-reported, small n, declining trend
  ($1,819 in 2022) — quote with care. (NFEC annual survey)
- Topic-level (Intuit Credit Karma/TurboTax, Mar 2026, n=1,095): wish school
  taught — investing 64%, budgeting 56%, credit cards 51%, debt 51%,
  **tax filing 50%**; **72% of Gen Z can't file taxes without help**; 88%
  say HS left them unprepared for real-world money.

### Revealed demand (huge, but free)
- #PersonalFinance **5.1B** TikTok views, #FinTok 4.7B (CNBC Jan 2024;
  hashtag counters since deprecated — order-of-magnitude only). #adulting
  ~8.8B (unofficial aggregator — don't cite externally).
- 76% of Gen Z gets money advice from social media; **83% have encountered
  misleading info there** (WallStreetZen 2023) → the trust wedge for our
  verified/citation-backed content.
- Google "How do I…" life-skill queries at all-time high, +25% YoY; Axios
  (May 2025): "Google is teaching American adults how to adult."

### Willingness to pay (real, conditional on outcomes)
- Dave Ramsey FPU: **$79.99 course × ~10M graduates** — the existence proof
  that US adults pay for structured financial education at scale.
- Books: *I Will Teach You to Be Rich* 1M+ copies; *Rich AF* NYT bestseller;
  *Financial Feminist* 150K copies year one.
- Creator paid layers: Caleb Hammer = YouTube's largest channel-membership
  operation; Sethi courses $29/mo–$797.
- The pattern: adults pay for **outcomes + accountability + personality**,
  not content. Pure "adulting school" subscriptions stayed niche ($19.99/mo,
  no winner); pure-education app subs collapse in price (Bloom $15→$5/mo).

### The K-12 mandate wave (tailwind, not threat)
- **30 states** guarantee a standalone HS personal finance course (NGPF,
  June 2026; only 11 fully implemented). CEE's broader count: 39 states.
- Mandates take effect with the **Classes of 2027–2031** (CA = 2031). In
  2020 only 8 states had guarantees; <1 in 4 students had access as late
  as 2021-22. **Today's 22–40-year-olds (HS ~2004–2022) are confirmed
  pre-mandate** — the market doesn't shrink until the mid-2030s.
- The annual legislation/news cycle (CEE survey releases, NGPF bill tracker,
  sponsored coverage) is recurring free category marketing aimed at adults.
- Adult literacy is **stagnant at 49%** correct, unchanged 2017→2025 (TIAA
  P-Fin Index).

### The academic decay argument (our positioning, peer-reviewed)
- Fernandes/Lynch/Netemeyer (2014, Management Science; 201 studies):
  financial-ed effects explain 0.1% of behavior variance and **decay to
  negligible within ~20 months**; authors explicitly recommend
  **"just-in-time financial education tied to the decision."**
- Kaiser/Lusardi/Menkhoff/Urban (2022, JFE; 76 RCTs): financial education
  *does* work (3× earlier effect estimates) — near-term. Decay stands.
- Net: even mandated graduates will need it again at the decision moment,
  5–15 years past the decay horizon. This is the honest answer to "but
  schools teach it now."

## 3. Competitive landscape — the lane is open

- **Frame owned by free content + creators with no product:** Khan Academy
  financial literacy (1M+ completions, sponsored), Zogo (1.5M bank-sponsored
  "users"), library/extension Adulting 101 (all free). Vivian Tu (8M
  followers — her stated mission is literally our line), Tori Dunlap,
  **Humphrey Yang (3.4M, says outright he has no product that fits his
  channel — partnership-shaped hole)**, newsletters (Dollar Scholar ~300K,
  Money with Katie).
- **Mechanics players don't serve paying US adults:** Greenlight/Bloom =
  kids/teens; Zogo = B2B-sponsored; Your Juno = free/UK; Finny — the actual
  "Duolingo for personal finance" — absorbed into B2B Origin (2023) with
  ~60K consumers; Fizz/"Mine" monetizes the card, education is bait, and is
  degrading.
- **Nobody combines school-gap frame + spaced repetition/retention/decision
  readiness for paying US adults.** Repeatedly proposed (HN/Indie Hackers),
  never shipped at scale. This is Numind's lane.
- Vanity-metric warning: no headline user count in this category (Zogo 1.5M,
  Step 7M, Khan 1M) represents paying adult learners.

## 4. Why the graveyard died (it wasn't timing)

| App | Death | Structural cause |
|---|---|---|
| Mint (free PFM) | shut Jan 2024 | Free → no revenue; data-monetization ceiling; Intuit harvested users into Credit Karma |
| Long Game | acquired/discontinued 2023 | "Learn/save gamified" had no outcome attached; sold to a bank as a feature |
| Zogo (as consumer) | pivoted B2B | Pennies-for-points rewards; sponsored model broke incentives |
| Finny | absorbed 2023 | Education-only subscription — knowledge has no deadline; CAC > LTV at ~60K users |
| Tally | dead Aug 2024 | Fintech balance-sheet economics (rate environment), not education |
| Bloom | price collapse $15→$5/mo | Education-only WTP is weak without an attached outcome/tool |

The common thread: **they sold knowledge, and knowledge has no deadline,
no receipt, and no daily trigger.** Survivors attach to money in motion
(YNAB = control, ~$49M ARR; Cleo = $250M+ ARR from credit products, not
education) or to outcomes with accountability (Ramsey). They were not
early — they were structurally unpaid. The fixes are exactly our
architecture: moments give deadlines, Readiness gives a receipt, Freshness
+ everyday reps give the daily trigger, honesty gives trust.

## 5. The daily-use question (puffcount/unrot-class apps)

Observation: feature-thin apps with aggressive TikTok marketing (quit-vaping
counters, doomscroll blockers, prayer locks) work because they attach to a
**daily-felt problem** and sell a **daily identity ritual**, not features.
Money *decisions* are episodic — but money *anxiety* and money *transactions*
are daily. Our daily case, in order of honesty:

1. **Daily transactions** — tips, checkout, unit prices, BNPL prompts,
   subscriptions. The workout's everyday-biased warm-up exists for this.
2. **Identity ritual** — the ring close is our "I'm someone who's on top of
   money" totem (the puffcount lesson: market the ritual, not the
   curriculum).
3. **Skill decay** — Freshness gives an honest re-engagement reason
   ("2 skills are fading") backed by the Fernandes decay literature.
4. **Dated moments** — when one is coming, daily reps have a visible
   destination (readiness progress toward Nov 1).
5. **Natural recurring triggers to build:** paycheck-day anchored sessions
   (every 2 weeks, universal), statement-day, enrollment windows. Notification
   copy tied to today's real transaction ("you'll see a tip screen today").

Realistic cadence: 4–6 closes/week (rest days exist by design), with
moment-spike reactivation — not Duolingo-style 365-day theater.

## 6. Our gaps against the promise (internal audit)

Lesson coverage vs the canonical checklist: strong on 18/25 (paycheck,
benefits, loans, housing, car, student loans, negotiation, scams…).
**Seven gaps — exactly the "administrative adulting" layer that research
says no product owns:**

1. Filing taxes mechanically (sharpest cited pain: 50% wish taught, 72% of
   Gen Z can't file alone) — we teach brackets, not filing
2. Building credit from zero (first/secured card, authorized user)
3. Disputing a charge / chargeback rights
4. Choosing/switching banks + avoiding account fees
5. Renters insurance
6. Auto insurance shopping (health is deep, auto absent)
7. Beneficiaries / basic estate hygiene

Mostly concept/decision lessons → also rebalances the 445-numerical /
304-concept skew. Positioning gaps: the de-math copy pass (math is the
method, never the promise) and the brand-line adoption.

## 7. Recommended next workstreams

1. **De-math copy pass** — funnel + product surfaces; "math" appears once,
   to disarm.
2. **Administrative-adulting content wave** — the 7 lessons above (+ pack
   placement: most fit Protect Your Money / First Apartment / First

3. **MomentCalendar soft-awareness rework** — "around the corner" nudges;
   no countdowns outside genuine windows.
4. **Paycheck-day trigger** — collect/infer pay cadence; anchor reminders
   to it.
5. **Brand-guidelines language rule** — codify the positioning sentence and
   the math-budget rule.
